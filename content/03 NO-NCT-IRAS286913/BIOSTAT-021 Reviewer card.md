# BIOSTAT-021 Reviewer card

## Review stance

Vision and balance changes after bilateral implantation of Toric IOLs provides a randomized controlled trial comparing Toric and non-Toric IOLs with identifiable population, intervention, and endpoint information. The statistical review classifies the SAP as Partial overall because several core statistical elements are either explicit, partial, missing, or not applicable depending on the question.

## Highest-priority talking points

- Clarify the estimand for the primary analysis.
- Confirm whether the sample size supports both effectiveness and safety interpretation.
- Confirm missing-data and intercurrent-event assumptions.
- Confirm whether multiplicity is controlled for all confirmatory claims.
- Check whether generalizability is justified for the intended device use environment.

## Questions to ask the sponsor or study team

- What exact treatment effect is the primary analysis estimating?
- Which analysis population supports the primary claim?
- What sensitivity analyses test missingness and protocol deviations?
- What safety event rate or confidence interval width can this study reasonably support?

## Claims that appear well supported

- Design: randomized controlled trial comparing Toric and non-Toric IOLs.
- Population: ambulatory adults aged over 50 with bilateral cataract and astigmatism over 1.0D.
- Treatment/device condition: Toric monofocal IOLs versus standard non-Toric monofocal IOLs.
- Endpoint: dynamic balance and related movement outcomes after cataract surgery.

## Claims needing caution

- Estimand completeness.
- Safety precision.
- Missing-data robustness.
- Multiplicity and exploratory endpoint interpretation.
- Generalizability beyond the observed study setting.
